The card game you are about to build

Fish shall NEVER play guess that card. A console program, a Magician, one hidden card, five tries. This brief says what the game is. How it is built is up to you.

You are going to build a console application: the player types a line, the program answers, until somebody wins. You will very likely build it with an LLM at your side. Fine — but the thinking is yours. You will form an idea of the game, turn the idea into a plan, and turn the plan into working, tested code. The LLM types faster than you. It does not get to decide for you.

This brief explains the game as a conversation first, then shows the exact commands that conversation becomes, then the rules, then what your tests must be able to prove.

1The game as a conversation

Read this first. Everything after it is the same thing expressed in commands.

- User: "I would like to guess a card."
- Magician: "I have drawn one card from my deck. It lies face down. You have five tries."
- User: "Queen of Hearts?"
- Magician: "No. Four tries left."
- User: "Queen of Spades?"
- Magician: "No. Three tries left."
- User: "King of Hearts?"
- Magician: "Correct." (turning over the King of Hearts) "You have won."

The one thing to understand before anything else

The Magician draws one card, at random, before the first guess — and then never touches the deck again. Every guess is aimed at that same face-down card. The card does not change between tries. The game is a duel between the player and a single fixed secret.

2The conversation, as exact commands

The conversationThe command
"Queen of Hearts?"Guess QH;
(leaving)EXIT

That is the whole command set. Guess ends with a semicolon. EXIT takes none — it is not a move in the game, it is the player leaving the table.

Do not build a clever parser. A command word, a card, a semicolon. If the input is stranger than that, what happens next is your design — see section 5.

Writing a card

Rank first, then suit, no space in between. Ranks: 2 through 10, then J, Q, K, A. Suits: H hearts, D diamonds, S spades, C clubs. So QH is the Queen of Hearts and 10D — three characters — is the ten of diamonds.

3The rules

  • The deck is 24 cards: 9 through A in all four suits. The hidden card is drawn from it at random when the game starts.
  • The player has 5 tries. Each Guess of a card consumes one.
  • A guess hits when it names exactly the hidden card — same rank and same suit. Anything else is a miss.
  • Any card may be guessed, even one that is not in the deck. Guess 2C; is a legal guess. It is also, against this deck, a guaranteed miss — noticing that is the player's job, not the program's.
  • Hit → the player wins. The game is over.
  • Five misses → the player loses. The Magician turns the card over: on defeat the hidden card is revealed. The game is over.
  • After the game is over, further Guess commands are ignored — the game state stops changing. Whether the program ignores them silently or says so is your design.
  • EXIT ends the program, at any moment.

4A session you could have

Below is one possible run. The wording is an example, not a requirement — your program may speak however you design it to speak. What it may not do is withhold the facts: section 7 lists exactly what a player must be able to tell from your console at every moment.

$ notforfish
The Magician has drawn a card. It lies face down. You have 5 tries.
> Guess QH;
Miss. 4 tries left.
> Guess QS;
Miss. 3 tries left.
> Guess KH;
Hit! The card was KH. You have won, in 3 tries.
> Guess AH;
(the game is over — nothing happens)
> EXIT

Your run will very probably look different, and not only in wording: the hidden card is random. This is one of several things that could have happened, not the thing that will happen.

5Decisions that are yours

This brief pins down what the game is. Everything it does not pin down is a design decision, and design decisions belong to you — not to the brief, and not to your LLM. Three you will meet early:

  • Strange input. Guess ZZ;, a missing semicolon, an empty line. Rejected? Reported? Does it consume a try? Your call — but make it a decision, not an accident.
  • The opening. What does the program say when it starts? How does a player who has never read this brief know what to type?
  • Repetition. The player guesses QH twice. The rules above make it a legal, wasted try. Do you warn them? Stop them? Let them burn?

Whatever you choose: write the choice down, and test it. "The LLM picked something" is not a decision.

6How a random game can be tested

Automated tests are part of this task, and here is the interesting part: the hidden card is random. How can a test assert "the player wins by guessing QH" when it cannot know the card?

It cannot — unless your program lets it. The game logic should never reach for randomness directly; it should ask for a card, from something a test can replace. Push the randomness to the edge, where it can be swapped out, and the game becomes completely predictable without a single rule changing. How you arrange that is your design. That your tests can arrange it is a requirement.

One session your test suite must be able to play:

StepEquivalent typingWhat is checked
pin(the test fixes the hidden card: QH)a fresh game, 0 of 5 tries used
try 1Guess 2C;miss — 1 try used, game still on
try 2Guess QH;hit — victory, on try 2
afterGuess KH;ignored — the game already ended, nothing changes

And one more, with no table given: a game that is lost. Five misses, defeat, the hidden card revealed. Deriving that session from the rules is your work; proving it with a test is the point.

7Done means

Every item below is something an observer can check at your console or by running your tests — nothing here is about how the code looks inside.

  • When the program starts, the player can tell a game is on and how many tries they have.
  • After every guess, the player can tell whether it hit or missed; after every miss, how many tries remain.
  • A win is unmistakable.
  • A loss is unmistakable, and reveals the hidden card.
  • After the game ends, further guesses visibly change nothing.
  • EXIT ends the program.
  • The automated tests pass, and they include: the pinned session from section 6, a lost game, and every decision you made in section 5.
  • The tests pin the hidden card without changing any game rule.
